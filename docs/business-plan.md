# Business Plan: North Star Holiday Lighting

*Permanent, app-controlled roofline lighting for homes in Minneapolis–St. Paul and the area within about 70 miles.*
*Legal entity: **North Star Lighting LLC**. Public name (Minnesota assumed name): **North Star Holiday Lighting**.*

## 1. The business in one paragraph

We install permanent RGB lighting in a slim aluminum channel under the eaves. It's color-matched to the trim, so it's nearly invisible by day. At night the homeowner controls it from a phone app: Christmas, Halloween, game day, the 4th, or warm white every evening. It's a one-time sale of **$2,500–$6,000+** per home, installed in one day by a two-person crew, with **55–65% gross margin**. Minnesota winters make the pitch easy: nobody wants to be on a roof in December or January.

## 2. Market and competition

**Service area:** Minneapolis–St. Paul plus about 70 miles: Hennepin, Ramsey, Dakota, Anoka, Washington, Scott, Carver and Wright counties, out to St. Cloud, Hudson WI, Northfield and Red Wing.

## 3. Product and sourcing

**System:** aluminum channel/track (color-matched), RGBW addressable pixel LEDs (IP67/IP68, rated to about −40°F), low-voltage power supplies, Wi-Fi controller and app, stainless fasteners.

**Sourcing options:**

| Option | Pros | Cons |
|---|---|---|
| **A. White-label / wholesale system** (recommended to start) | Your brand on everything, best margins, no franchise fees | You choose the supplier and stand behind the warranty |
| B. Authorized dealer of a national brand | Brand recognition, marketing support | Dealer fees and minimums, territory rules, less margin, their name not yours |

**Before choosing a supplier, check:** cold rating (−40°F), ingress rating (IP67+), a ≥5-year product warranty passed through to you, a US-based support line, the app's ratings and stability, stock on hand in the US, and channel colors that match common Minnesota trim (white, almond, bronze, black, clay).

## 7. Licensing and compliance (Minnesota)

- **⚠️ Electrical licensing, verify first.** Minnesota regulates low-voltage (power-limited) installations more than most states. Call the **Minnesota Department of Labor & Industry (DLI), Electrical Licensing**, and ask whether permanent low-voltage lighting that plugs into an existing outlet needs a **Power Limited Technician** license, an electrical contractor license, or a permit. Get the answer in writing before your first install. Plan for a licensed partner, or a PLT license for yourself or an employee if required.
- Any new outlet or circuit is done by a licensed electrician (pass-through cost).
- City business licenses where required. Check HOA rules on each job; most HOAs approve channel-mounted lighting.
- Sales tax: collect and remit Minnesota sales tax as required for your contract type. Ask your accountant how lighting installs are classified.

## 8. Operations

**The year:**

| Months | Focus |
|---|---|
| Jan–Mar | Indoor work: supplier orders, hiring, training, demo-board shows at home & garden shows (Minneapolis Home + Garden Show) |
| Apr–Jun | Off-season installs (7% savings), door-to-door canvassing in target suburbs, HOA and builder partnerships |
| Jul–Aug | Peak selling starts: "Install before Halloween" campaign |
| Sep–Nov | **Peak install season.** Two crews if possible. Halloween and Christmas deadlines drive demand |
| Dec | Final installs on dry days, referral asks, holiday photos of every install |

**Install day (most homes):** 2-person crew, about 6–8 hours on site. Measure, cut and pre-wire the channel, mount it to the fascia or soffit with stainless screws, mount the power supply near an outlet, set up the controller and app, then a 15-minute walkthrough with the homeowner.

**Safety:** fall protection on any roof steeper than 6/12 and all second-story work. Ladder stabilizers. No work on icy or wet roofs.

**Service:** respond within 48 hours. Keep spare pixel sections and power supplies on the van.

## 9. Marketing plan

1. **Your own house first.** Install a full system at home and post every holiday scene.
2. **Yard signs at every install** for two weeks: "Lights by North Star."
3. **Door hangers** on the 10 houses on each side of every install, with a $250 neighbor offer.
4. **Google Business Profile.** Photos of every install at dusk, and a review request the night of the install.
5. **Facebook and Instagram:** one install per week, day vs. night, with the scene name (e.g. "Purple and gold in Plymouth"). Short videos of the app switching scenes do best.
6. **Door-to-door canvassing (Apr–Oct).** Show the demo board on an iPad. This is the main sales engine in this industry.
7. **Partnerships:** custom home builders, roofers and gutter companies (referral fee 5–10%), realtors (listing photos), HOAs (whole-neighborhood packages).
8. **Events:** home and garden shows and the State Fair (later). A lit demo board stops people.
9. **Paid search:** Google Local Services Ads plus search on "permanent Christmas lights Minneapolis." Start at $30–50/day from August to November.

**Target customer:** homeowners aged 35–65 with household income above $125k in suburbs and exurbs, especially two-story homes, families with kids, and people who already hire seasonal installers.

## 11. Risks and mitigations

| Risk | Mitigation |
|---|---|
| Licensing surprise (DLI) | Get a written answer before launch. Partner with a licensed electrician or PLT |
| Product failures in extreme cold | Choose a supplier with a −40°F rating and a pass-through warranty. Keep spares. 2% warranty reserve |

| Falls and injury | Fall protection, training, workers' comp |
| Competition from national dealers | Local story, speed, service, neighbor pricing |
