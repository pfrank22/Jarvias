# Business Plan: Residential Christmas Light Installation

*Working name: **Evergleam Holiday Lighting** (placeholder: see the launch kit for alternatives and a name-check checklist).*
*Assumptions: a mid-size US suburban market, a 2–3 person crew, and an owner with a truck. All figures are typical US ranges as of 2026. Check local prices before you commit money.*

---

## 1. The opportunity in one paragraph

Homeowners want a beautiful display but don't want to climb ladders, untangle lights, or store bins of cords. A professional installer **leases** commercial-grade LED lights, cut to fit each house. It installs them in one visit, fixes problems free all season, then takes them down and stores them. Because the lights are reused for 3+ seasons, gross margins run **55–65%**, and about **70–80% of customers renew** every year. That makes the revenue predictable. The season is short (about October 1 to January 15), so it works well as a side business, a seasonal division, or a winter add-on for landscapers, roofers, and exterior trades.

**Fast-start checklist for the next 7 days**
1. Form an LLC and get an EIN (1–3 days online).
2. Get general liability insurance: quotes the same day from Next, Hiscox, or a local agent.
3. Order C9 LED stringers and bulbs (warm white plus multicolor), clips, and timers from a wholesale supplier. Wholesalers ship in 2–5 days; Amazon is a backup.
4. Publish the website in `site/`. Set up a free Google Business Profile.
5. Post a "now booking, X spots left before Christmas" offer in local groups. Knock doors on 2–3 streets that already decorate heavily.
6. Quote with the calculator (`site/index.html?owner` shows your margin).

## 5. Operations

**Season calendar**

| When | What |
|---|---|
| Jan–Feb | Takedown (by about January 15), inventory audit, renewal agreements for next season |
| Mar–Jul | Off-season: buy inventory on sale, hire, train, and optionally sell permanent track lighting (a year-round upsell) |
| Aug | Renewal outreach to past customers, early-bird campaign |
| Sep | Quoting season. Measure homes, book install dates, finish prep cutting |
| Oct 15 – Dec 15 | **Install season**: 2–4 homes per crew per day |
| Dec | Service calls (respond within 48 h), wrap up final installs |
| Jan 2–15 | Takedown, back to customer bins, storage |

**Crew productivity (planning numbers)**
- 3-person crew installs an average home in 2–4 hours, or 2–3 homes per day including drive time.
- Takedown is about 40% of install time.
- Over 50 install days, one crew can handle about **100–120 homes**.

**Safety (non-negotiable)**
- Ladder training. Stabilizers on every extension ladder. Three points of contact at all times.
- Harness and roof anchor on any roof steeper than 6/12 or any second-story roof.
- No work on frosty or wet roofs or in high wind. Reschedule instead.
- Use GFCI-protected outlets only. Never exceed about 80% of a circuit's load. Keep connections off the ground and away from water.
- Don't hardwire anything. In most areas, new outlets must be installed by a licensed electrician; refer this work out or partner with one.

**Licensing:** plug-in decorative lighting usually needs only a general business license. **Check your city and state** for contractor registration rules, and for any electrical licensing triggered by adding circuits.

## 6. Marketing plan

Ranked by return for a new residential installer:

1. **Your installs are your ads.** Put a yard sign at every job ("Lights by Evergleam · scan for a quote"). Neighbors ask the homeowner who did it.
2. **Street clustering.** After each install, leave door hangers at the 10 houses on either side and offer a neighbor discount. This also reduces drive time.
3. **Google Business Profile.** Add photos of every install and ask every customer for a review the night of the install, while they're excited.
4. **Nextdoor and local Facebook groups.** Post before-and-after photos with an offer.
5. **Renewals.** Text past customers in August with "lock in your spot" and the early-bird price.
6. **Partnerships.** Realtors (closing gifts), HOAs (matching neighborhood displays), and landscapers or gutter cleaners (referral fee about 10%).
7. **Paid ads (optional).** Google Local Services Ads or Meta ads targeted to a 10-mile radius, homeowners, and higher-income ZIP codes. Start at $20–40 per day from October through early December.

**Target customer:** homeowners aged 35–70 with household income above $100k, in subdivisions with HOAs or established neighborhoods. Also busy two-income families, older homeowners who no longer climb ladders, and homes for sale in December.

**Cash flow:** collect a **50% deposit at booking** and the remaining 50% on install day. That deposit pays for your inventory before you hang it.

## 8. Risks and mitigations

| Risk | Mitigation |
|---|---|
| Falls / injury | Safety rules above, harnesses, and insurance including workers' comp |
| Weather delays | Build 20% slack into the schedule and promise a date range, not an exact day |
| Property damage claims | Clips only (never nails or staples). Take photos before and after every install |
| Seasonal cash crunch | Deposits, early-bird pricing, and permanent lighting sales in the off-season |
| Price competition | Compete on service (48-hour fixes, storage, design), not price. Don't race to the bottom |
| Customer-owned lights failing | Hang them at labor rate with no performance guarantee, and offer an upgrade |

## 9. Growth path

1. **Year 1:** 1 crew, 50–80 homes, collect reviews and photos.
2. **Year 2:** 2 crews, 120–180 homes. Add **permanent RGB track lighting** ($25–40/ft installed, sold all year).
3. **Year 3:** Add small commercial work (storefronts, offices, HOA entrances) and event lighting. Consider a franchise or a dealer network for supplies.
